# GPUI bump to zed@703b5ac3 (2026-11-16)

The pin moves from `37faef81` to `703b5ac3` on `feat/gpui-703b5ac3`
(`tasks/prd-gpui-703b5ac3-upgrade.md`). The commit before the bump is
`9f606d3aa` (GPUI `37faef81`); the branch head when this summary was written
is `459f2b36d`. This file gathers what the bump measured and changed. The
Linux suites, the timing re-baseline, the Linux latency and the unfocused
frame rate were measured on Fedora on 2026-11-16 by US-027 and US-024 (PRD
1.5); the final branch commit they measure is `d78d92927`.

### The bump against the committed baselines and the old pin

**Terminal.** Every timing is within 10 % of the `9f606d3aa` runs and of the
`92a5c29d` baseline, apart from two 10 ns-resolution medians that are bimodal on
both pins: `line_text_at_220x60` reads about 360 or about 440 ns (the old pin read
440 twice on one earlier pair, the bump 380 to 460) and `base_font_resolve` 30
or 40 ns, one tick of the timer. Neither is a code change. The slowest
moves above that resolution are `service_spaces_220x60` (+9.0 % on both
runs) and `service_text_220x60` (19.7 and 20.8 us against 18.6, +6.2 % and
+11.9 %, +6.5 % above the baseline on the pair mean); no service code or
parser changed with the bump.
`pipeline_corpus_mib_s` is 1.84 MiB/s against 1.78 before.

**`layout_echo_shifted_uncached_220x60`.** The suite's own 1.10x p95 budget
against `layout_echo_unshifted_uncached_220x60` holds on Fedora: 1.03 and 1.01
on the bump, 1.03 and 1.03 on the old pin. The 1.22x read under WSL2 (US-003)
was the WSL2 run, not the bump.

**Editor: a regression from US-013, found here and fixed.** On the first quiet
runs of the bump (`2f8a3d376`, [editor-20261116T183929Z-2f8a3d376aa0.json](editor-20261116T183929Z-2f8a3d376aa0.json),
[editor-20261116T183939Z-2f8a3d376aa0.json](editor-20261116T183939Z-2f8a3d376aa0.json))
opening a file was up to 1.9x slower than on the old pin:

| Metric | `9f606d3aa` | `2f8a3d376` | `d78d92927` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `open_300kb_highlighted` | 153 us | 276 us | 157 us |
| `open_2mb_to_text` | 1.04 ms | 1.89 ms | 1.07 ms |
| `open_3_7mb` | 1.86 ms | 3.41 ms | 1.92 ms |
| `open_markdown_injected` | 34 us | 65 us | 36 us |
| `reload_10_hunks_100k_lines_ui` | 19.9 us | 25.6 us | 20.6 us |

Cause: US-013 measured the longest line of an opened file through a per-byte
tab fold, where the old code split on newlines and took each length, and the
columns of every edited row through a per-character Unicode width lookup,
where the old code read the row's character count from the rope's metadata.
With the measure replaced by a constant in a copy of each pin, both pins open
in the same time within 2 %, so the whole gap was the measure. `d78d92927`
(US-027) takes the byte-length split again when every byte is printable ASCII
or a newline, which one vectorized pass checks, and folds ASCII bytes without
the width table everywhere else; three equivalence tests hold the new measure
to the per-character fold, across rope chunks and on ASCII, tab, control and
non-ASCII text. Every editor timing is now within 4 % of the old pin, apart
from `prepaint_60_rows_warm`.

**`prepaint_60_rows_warm`: accepted.** 24.6 us against 22.8 us on the old pin
(+8 %) and 22.0 us in the `92a5c29d` baseline (+12 %). The old pin itself reads
4 % above that baseline. The rest is US-013: on a warm cache the editor now
clones a shaped line that carries its tab stops, about 2 us per 60-row viewport
against a 16.7 ms frame. It shows with the measure replaced by a constant
too (+7 %), so it does not come from the longest-line measure. Arthur accepted
it on 2026-11-16.

### Allocation baselines

`scripts/perf-gates.sh --refresh-alloc-baselines` on `d78d92927` moved
`terminal-alloc.json` (was `bb8621dc`) and `editor-alloc.json` (was
`988bd78f`) to `d78d92927`. One column moved by more than 1 %:
`keystroke_3_7mb_plain` goes from 4.0 to 4.05 allocations per iteration (1213 to
1216.2 bytes), on every bump run since US-013 and on none of the old pin's.
Cause: the columns of the edited row are now read by walking its chunks, and
ropey 1.6.1 gives a row held in one leaf a light slice whose chunk iterator
does not allocate, but a row that straddles two leaves a full slice whose
iterator allocates its node stack (`ropey-1.6.1/src/iter.rs:1406`). About one
row in 20 of the 3.7 MB corpus straddles two leaves. Before US-013 the
character count came from the rope's metadata and walked nothing.

## Path costs (US-003)

Measured under WSL2 on the baseline CPU, not on Fedora
([paths summary](gpui-703b5ac3-paths-2026-11-15.md)), release profile, 300
iterations per case at scale 2:

| Metric | `37faef81` p50 | `703b5ac3` p50 | Time | Alloc per iteration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `paths_tab_strip_12_badges` | 32.0 to 32.3 us | 159.2 to 161.4 us | about 5x | 891 KiB to 394 KiB |
| `paths_curly_underline_200x50` | 9.29 to 9.60 ms | 10.97 to 11.22 ms | +14 % to +21 % | 67.14 MiB to 21.88 MiB |
| `paths_box_drawing_200x50` | 27.37 to 28.34 ms | 30.86 to 30.88 ms | +9 % to +13 % | 97.02 MiB to 18.06 MiB |

**Squircle budget: within.** The 12-badge strip costs 0.16 ms per frame at the
median and 0.27 ms at the worst p95, under the 0.4 ms budget, so no shape cache
exists: every squircle is built at its final bounds on every paint (US-004).
The old badge figure is not comparable: the old renderer let Paneflow build a
shape once and translate it, which `703b5ac3` no longer allows.

### Metrics more than 10 % above the previous renderer

| Metric | Change (run means) | Cause | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| `paths_curly_underline_200x50` | +17 % under WSL2, +14 % on Fedora | GPUI `703b5ac3` turns each stroke into an outline through `tiny_skia_path` and bins it into tiles like a fill on every paint (`zed/crates/gpui/src/path_builder.rs:306-364`, #66809: "strokes are 2-8x slower"); 50 strokes of 400 cubic segments per frame | **Accepted** by Arthur on 2026-11-16 (US-027) |
| `paths_box_drawing_200x50` | +11 % under WSL2, +17 % on Fedora | the same stroke path, one stroke per arc or diagonal, two per cross, over 10,000 cells | **Accepted** by Arthur on 2026-11-16 (US-027) |

The mitigation left for later is to rasterize each box-drawing glyph and each
undercurl cell once into GPUI's sprite atlas, as Ghostty does, so that a frame
paints sprites instead of building and binning strokes. Both cases are
full-screen worst cases; a real TUI draws arcs at frame corners and undercurls
a few diagnostics, so a normal frame pays a small fraction of them. Memory per
frame falls 3 to 5 times on the new renderer. US-027 re-measured both on Fedora
(the "Terminal strokes against the previous renderer on Fedora" section
above).

## Frame intervals (US-009)

GPUI `703b5ac3` limits every redraw, not only forced renders and next-frame
callbacks as `37faef81` did (`zed/crates/gpui/src/window.rs:1977`, #66237).
Both Paneflow windows now state the policy instead of inheriting it
(`src-app/src/frame_policy.rs`, `paced_window_options`):

| Condition | Interval | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Window without the keyboard focus | 33.333 ms | 30 fps, lifted during sustained high-rate input such as a scroll |
| Platform low-power mode | 33.333 ms | 30 fps |
| Serious or critical thermal pressure | 16.667 ms | 60 fps |

The longest interval that holds wins, and a mandatory platform presentation
bypasses all three. The values equal GPUI's defaults at `703b5ac3`.

**User-visible change:** an unfocused Paneflow window, such as a detached pane
on another display, updates streaming output at most 30 times per second. On
Windows it presented 68 to 70 frames per second on the old pin and 28.3 on the
new one, never more than 30 in a whole second
([frame pacing summary](gpui-703b5ac3-frame-pacing-2026-11-15.md)). On
Fedora (GNOME 51.1, Wayland, US-024) it presented 90.8 to 92.3 frames per
second on the old pin and 25.4 and 25.7 on the new one, never more than 27 in
a whole second.

On Windows low power is read only from `GUID_POWER_SAVING_STATUS`; Energy
Saver on this desktop sets `GUID_ENERGY_SAVER_STATUS` only, so the low-power
cap never engages here (unchanged since the `37faef81` bump).

## Keystroke latency (US-009, US-010)

Windows 11, Ryzen 7 7800X3D, RTX 4070 Ti SUPER, 2560 by 1440 at 240 Hz, debug
builds of both pins with the latency probe logging every keystroke, 200
keystrokes per run. The figure is `key-handler→paint-cpu`, from the key handler
to the end of the CPU paint of the next terminal frame; it excludes GPU
presentation and the display.

| Pin | Runs | Median (mean of run medians) | p95 (mean) |
|---|---|---|---|
| old `37faef81` | 2 | 4.99 ms | 7.10 ms |
| new `703b5ac3` | 3 | 5.57 ms | 7.67 ms |

**Within the 2 ms budget**: 0.58 ms slower at the median, less than one 240 Hz
frame (4.2 ms). Its cause was not profiled.

- **Linux (US-024):** Fedora 45, GNOME 51.1 on Wayland, NVIDIA GeForce RTX
  4070 Ti SUPER on the 620.70.06 driver (open kernel modules), Vulkan, two
  2560 by 1440 displays at scale 1. The old pin's five runs read a 3.05 ms
  median (p95 3.63 ms), the new pin's six 3.12 ms (p95 3.70 ms): **0.07 ms
  apart, within the 2 ms budget.** Procedure and every run in the
  [frame pacing summary](gpui-703b5ac3-frame-pacing-2026-11-15.md).
- **macOS: not measured.** No Mac is available before 2027, so US-010 is
  cancelled (PRD 1.2). The macOS build is validated by CI only, and the
  key-to-screen latency report of zed-industries/zed#66954 (about 18 ms more at
  120 Hz after `0373366f43`) is accepted on trust in the upstream code. The
  hardware procedure stays in the frame pacing summary.

## Rendering facts checked on hardware

- **Linux translucent windows (US-008):** translucent squircles drawn as paths
  over a translucent window blend correctly on GNOME (Mutter) and KDE Plasma
  (KWin), each on Wayland and X11, with the NVIDIA driver and with Mesa AMD
  (RADV), checked by Arthur on Fedora on 2026-11-15. The rounded-quad fallback
  is removed.
- **Windows bordered shapes (US-006):** visual pass at scale 1 on 2026-11-15;
  scale 2 is not checked, both displays of this host run at scale 1.
